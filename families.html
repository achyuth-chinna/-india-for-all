Tax inside an everyday bill

A ₹1,180 tax-inclusive bill at 18% contains ₹180 GST, not ₹212.40. Formula: billed amount × rate ÷ (100 + rate). Not every item has GST: some supplies are exempt or nil-rated, and other taxes may apply. Always read the actual bill.

A sample monthly basket of ₹10,000 billed at 5% plus ₹5,000 billed at 18% contains about ₹1,239 GST, or ₹14,867 over 12 identical months. These are assumed receipts, not official tax rates for groceries or every household.

Lower bills for families still require public funding

If a public service covered every expense in the lifetime illustration, gross household bills avoided would be about ₹7.36–₹34.88 lakh per person. This is an upper coverage scenario, not a promised entitlement or net saving.

Taxes, government costs, quality, waiting times, travel and services not covered all matter. Net savings cannot be honestly calculated without a funded policy. Public education and healthcare also need teachers, clinicians, buildings, medicines and accountability.

These are possible pathways, not guaranteed outcomes. Education and health do not automatically make anyone disciplined or ethical, eliminate crime, or make every family happy. Rights, fair institutions and individual choices matter too.

Demand better spending, with evidence

Ask what a scheme costs, who benefits, whether it works and what audits found. Food security, disability support and pensions can complement education and health. A scheme is not wasteful merely because it is free.

A documented example, not a blanket accusation

CAG PMKVY 20/2025: ₹12.16 crore interest recovered; ₹24.13 crore administrative overcharge reported (para 4.3.1). Read government responses.

Recovered money is not a new recurring budget. Audit findings are not automatically proven fraud or cash available to fund universal services. Fix documented leakage while protecting essential support.
